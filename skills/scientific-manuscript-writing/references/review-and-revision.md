# Review And Revision

## Five Review Roles

### Author

- Is the central contribution stated consistently in title, abstract, introduction, results, discussion, and conclusion?
- Does every main claim have evidence and a visible location?
- Are limitations stated at the point where they matter?

### Expert Reviewer

- Are the model, approximations, parameters, and extraction procedures reproducible?
- Could an alternative mechanism explain the observation?
- Are thresholds, fits, and calibrations defined independently of the desired conclusion?
- Are comparisons with prior art technically fair?

### Editor

- Is the paper's question apparent early?
- Does each main figure advance a distinct part of the argument?
- Is the claimed contribution proportionate to the evidence and journal scope?

### Non-Specialist Scientific Reader

- Are the system, perturbation, and measured observable identifiable without field-specific assumptions?
- Can the physical change be seen in the figures without reading dense in-panel text?
- Are specialist terms introduced only after their physical role is clear?

### Production Reviewer

- Do cross-references, bibliography, units, symbols, affiliations, funding, and author information compile correctly?
- Are figures legible at final size and consistent in font, axis, line width, and color?
- Does the PDF render correctly on desktop, phone, and print-like grayscale views?

## Revision Order

Revise in descending order of leverage:

1. unsupported or overstated claims;
2. wrong or incomplete physical logic;
3. duplicated or missing structural steps;
4. figure-evidence mismatch;
5. terminology and notation;
6. sentence rhythm and grammar;
7. formatting and production details.

Do not spend a full pass polishing sentences that may disappear after structural revision.

## Versioning

Create a major snapshot for changes to the central claim, model, dataset, figure logic, section structure, or journal format. Overwrite the current working output for narrow wording, citation, or formatting corrections. Follow the project's own policy when it is more specific.

## Completion Record

After a revision, report:

- scientific changes;
- editorial changes;
- files or figures affected;
- calculations or builds performed;
- unresolved assumptions or evidence gaps;
- whether the rendered deliverable was inspected.
