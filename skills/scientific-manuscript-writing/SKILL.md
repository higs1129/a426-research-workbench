# Scientific Manuscript Writing

## Purpose and boundaries

Build a scientific argument whose claims, evidence, physical reasoning, and visual presentation remain traceable. Prefer a coherent line of reasoning over section-by-section reporting.

Project-specific instructions, journal requirements, verified data, and author decisions override the generic defaults in this skill.

This skill develops a manuscript argument from authorized evidence. It does not independently establish experimental facts, verify every literature claim without source access, replace venue instructions, or authorize submission.

## When to use

- Use for manuscript planning, drafting, restructuring, revision, figure/equation integration, claim-evidence auditing, or theory-experiment alignment.
- Use when a paper's scientific logic, terminology, novelty boundary, evidence chain, or rendered deliverable needs coordinated review.
- Do not use for literature retrieval alone, a standalone peer-review report, a single paper summary, slide/poster preparation, or general promotional copy.

## Reproducible workflow

### 1. Start with evidence

1. Locate and read the journal instructions, project profile, claim-evidence matrix, source data, prior manuscripts, and author-approved terminology.
2. State four working sentences before drafting:
   - what is established;
   - what limitation or unresolved question remains;
   - what this work contributes;
   - what the present evidence does and does not establish.
3. Build or update a claim-evidence matrix before making quantitative or novelty claims. Use [references/claim-evidence.md](references/claim-evidence.md).
4. Flag missing provenance, calibration, repeatability, or definitions. Do not repair gaps with confident prose.

### 2. Build the argument

Organize the paper around a scientific question, not around the order of laboratory operations or figure numbers. A useful causal chain is:

`system -> perturbation -> physical mechanism -> observable -> analysis -> bounded conclusion`

Each paragraph should perform one job and create the reason for the next paragraph. Introduce the system before its limitation, the limitation before the proposed observable, and the observable before its quantitative validation. See [references/structure-and-prose.md](references/structure-and-prose.md).

### 3. Connect theory and experiment

Use a repeated theory-experiment loop:

1. Define the physical quantity and its regime of validity.
2. Derive or calculate an observable consequence.
3. Identify the measured counterpart and its extraction method.
4. Compare at the level justified by calibration and uncertainty.
5. State whether the comparison is quantitative agreement, consistency in trend, or qualitative support.

Never force an exact match by redefining a threshold, selecting a convenient operating point without disclosure, or treating a fit-derived feature as a directly resolved feature.

### 4. Use figures, equations, and citations deliberately

- Give every main figure one scientific question and one take-home conclusion.
- Let the visible change carry the argument; use labels to identify quantities, not to narrate paragraphs inside the figure.
- Introduce an equation only when it defines a quantity, exposes a mechanism or scale, connects observables, or supports a calculation used later.
- Cite the exact claim supported by the source. Prefer primary literature for novelty, mechanisms, and quantitative precedent.
- Distinguish measured, fitted, calculated, inferred, and extrapolated quantities in text and captions.

Read [references/figures-equations-citations.md](references/figures-equations-citations.md) when planning figures, selecting equations, or revising literature framing.

### 5. Write in scientific language

- Prefer domain-standard physical nouns and verbs over generic engineering or promotional language.
- Define a symbol once and use it consistently.
- Use the same term for the same observable across text, equations, captions, and figures.
- Replace vague intensity words such as "strong," "excellent," or "clear" with a measurable regime or visible consequence when possible.
- Report statistical metrics only when they answer a scientific question; do not let fit scores replace physical interpretation.
- Preserve uncertainty and limitations without turning every sentence into a disclaimer.

### 6. Review in passes

Do not attempt a single all-purpose polish. Review in this order:

1. scientific claims and evidence;
2. causal structure and paragraph transitions;
3. terminology, symbols, units, and methods;
4. figures, captions, equations, and cross-references;
5. citations and novelty boundaries;
6. language, production, compilation, and mobile/print readability.

Adopt separate author, expert-reviewer, editor, non-specialist-reader, and production-review perspectives. Use [references/review-and-revision.md](references/review-and-revision.md).

### 7. Deliver revisions

- Preserve the project's versioning and build policy.
- Separate major scientific or structural revisions from small wording fixes.
- After editing, compile or render the actual deliverable and inspect it rather than assuming source changes rendered correctly.
- Summarize what changed scientifically, what remains uncertain, and what was verified.
- Keep project-specific guidance in the project, not in this reusable skill.

## Evidence and validation

Before treating a draft or revision as complete:

- every central claim has a visible evidence source, processing route, assumptions, uncertainty status, and allowed wording;
- measured, fitted, calculated, inferred, predicted, and extrapolated quantities remain distinct in text, figures, captions, and tables;
- novelty language has been checked against appropriate primary sources and narrowed to what the evidence supports;
- symbols, units, terminology, thresholds, calibration definitions, figure references, and quantitative values agree across the manuscript;
- alternative explanations, regime limits, missing repeatability, and unresolved evidence gaps are stated where they affect interpretation;
- the actual deliverable has been compiled or rendered and inspected at publication, phone, and print-like scale when relevant;
- the completion record identifies scientific changes, editorial changes, affected files, performed checks, and unresolved items.

## Failure modes

- Polishing sentences before resolving unsupported claims, physical logic, or figure-evidence mismatch.
- Organizing Results around figure numbers or laboratory chronology instead of a scientific question and causal chain.
- Presenting fitted, smoothed, inferred, or extrapolated behavior as directly observed.
- Selecting a threshold, operating point, fit range, or comparison because it makes the preferred conclusion look stronger.
- Using citations decoratively or relying on titles/abstracts for quantitative, mechanistic, or priority claims.
- Allowing equations, notation, captions, and prose to define the same quantity differently.
- Treating a successful compile as scientific validation, or editing project-specific requirements into the reusable Skill.
