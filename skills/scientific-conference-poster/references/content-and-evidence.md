# Content and evidence workflow

Read this reference during initial intake, source review, and content-priority evaluation. Stop using it once the priority proposal is confirmed unless new evidence changes the poster story.

## Minimal intake

When context is absent, ask for conference identity, abstract or abstract location, presentation form, and the authoritative material location in one concise message. Presentation form includes the physical or digital medium, size and orientation, language, official template, required files, deadline, and any accompanying oral introduction.

If the user supplies a material folder instead of pasting the abstract, inspect the folder first. Do not make them reproduce content that can be read from the authorized source.

## Source inventory

Read only the materials relevant to the poster and record:

| Area | Look for | Report when unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| Conference | official instructions, board size, submission format, deadline | dated, missing, or conflicting rules |
| Scientific story | accepted abstract, manuscript, report, meeting notes | different claims or versions |
| Authorship | accepted title, author order, affiliations, presenter, contact | spelling, ordering, or version conflicts |
| Evidence | figures, data, captions, statistics, uncertainty, units | missing source, unclear processing, unsupported number |
| Visual assets | device photos, schematics, plots, original vector or high-resolution subfigures, logos | unknown provenance, target panel, permission, crop, or resolution |
| Supporting matter | references, funding, acknowledgements, QR destination | incomplete or unverified details |
| Prior style | earlier poster, slide deck, institutional guide | whether it is a requirement or only a reference |

Use explicit source locations that another reviewer can reopen. Keep observation, author instruction, inference, and recommendation distinguishable.

## Authoritative title and identity

Use the confirmed accepted abstract as the title authority. The poster title must match it after normalizing only whitespace and line breaks. Preserve wording, symbols, punctuation, capitalization, and scientific qualifiers. When several abstract files or revisions disagree, show the competing titles and ask which one was accepted before creating the wireframe.

Record author order, author markers, affiliation mapping, presenter, corresponding author, contact details, and any equal-contribution notation as separate fields. Represent the presenter inside the author list with red, bold, underlined text according to [poster-visual-system.md](poster-visual-system.md). Keep session, contact, and venue metadata only when supported by the source or required by the meeting.

For every intended scientific image, inventory the best available asset separately from the composite figure in which it may appear. Record the target subpanel, required axes, scale bar, legend, colorbar, annotations, and whether an original EPS, SVG, PDF, high-resolution raster, or editable plot source is available. Route composite-panel extraction to [figure-extraction-and-placement.md](figure-extraction-and-placement.md).

## Questions after reading

Ask only questions the sources cannot answer. High-value prompts include:

- “If the audience remembers one statement, what should it be?”
- “Which result or object should dominate the page?”
- “Which figures or messages are mandatory, and which can be supporting or omitted?”
- “Should the poster explain itself without you, or support a live explanation?”
- “Who is the primary audience, and what do they already know?”

Do not replace the author's answer with the most visually dramatic available figure.

## Priority proposal

Use five relative classes:

1. **Primary message** — the one claim or outcome that governs the poster.
2. **Hero evidence** — the visual or result that most directly supports it.
3. **Supporting evidence** — results needed to make the main claim credible or bounded.
4. **Context and navigation** — minimum method, system, or background needed to interpret the evidence.
5. **Identity and traceability** — authorship, contact, references, acknowledgements, sources, and required marks.

List candidates to merge, shorten, defer to supplementary material, or omit separately. Omission is a communication recommendation, not deletion of source material.

A useful review table is:

| Candidate content | Scientific role | Authoritative source | Proposed priority | Visual treatment | Open issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Avoid assigning exact area percentages until the page dimensions, figure shapes, and style direction are known. When suggesting approximate area later, explain the comparison it supports rather than presenting the number as a standard.

## Confirmation gate

Before style discussion, summarize:

- the accepted abstract title and any unresolved version conflict;
- the presenter and confirmed author-affiliation mapping;
- the proposed remembered statement;
- the primary visual and supporting evidence chain;
- material proposed for reduction or omission;
- unresolved claims, data, credits, or requirements.

Ask the user to correct or approve this content hierarchy. Style must not be used to conceal an unresolved scientific decision.
