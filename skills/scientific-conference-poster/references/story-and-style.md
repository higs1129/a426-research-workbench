# Story and style brief

Read this reference after the content priorities have been proposed. The purpose is to translate an approved scientific hierarchy into a visual direction without forcing the user to invent design vocabulary.

## Story structure

Choose a reading structure that fits the evidence rather than defaulting to paper sections. Common structures include:

- one hero result with a short evidence path around it;
- problem → system or method → result → implication;
- two coordinated research branches converging on one conclusion;
- comparison between devices, conditions, methods, or generations;
- platform overview supported by several bounded performance demonstrations.

The structure should answer:

1. What attracts attention from a distance?
2. What does the viewer understand during a short browse?
3. What details support a technical conversation at close range?

## Derive before asking

Extract fixed or suggested choices from conference rules, institutional branding, prior posters, and the shapes of the approved figures. Separate requirements from references. Do not ask the user to choose what is already mandated.

## Fixed personal defaults

Read and apply [poster-visual-system.md](poster-visual-system.md) before asking style questions. Treat its enlarged typography and relative-size ladder, black native text, semantic spacing rhythm, black/red/blue/green active palette, inline presenter styling, and flat low-border composition as the default contract. Do not ask the user to rechoose font scale, an unrestricted palette, or card styling unless venue requirements conflict or the user explicitly wants an override.

The style conversation should resolve composition, density, character, semantic assignment within the fixed palette, branding placement, and deliberate departures from previous posters. Any proposed departure should be named explicitly in the style brief rather than introduced silently during production.

## Style axes

Ask only about unresolved axes:

- **character:** restrained academic, institutional formal, technical and engineered, or visually expressive;
- **density:** rapid scan, balanced, or detail-rich for close discussion;
- **composition:** one dominant hero, two coordinated branches, or modular open groups with minimal dividers;
- **continuity:** preserve an earlier visual system, selectively reuse it, or establish a new one;
- **color:** semantic assignment within black, red, blue, and green; required brand-color exceptions; grayscale and color-vision needs;
- **language:** Chinese, English, or a deliberate division of roles rather than duplicated bilingual headings;
- **editing:** whether collaborators need to rearrange native objects after delivery.

When the user has no strong preference, offer two or three directions grounded in the material. Each direction should describe hierarchy, rhythm, color role, and likely tradeoff in one short paragraph. Do not generate polished artwork merely to ask which style they prefer.

## Style brief output

Record:

- intended audience and viewing situation;
- visual character and density;
- composition model and reading order;
- color, typography, semantic spacing, language, and branding constraints;
- required delivery and later-editing format;
- reference material to continue or deliberately depart from;
- choices still deferred to the wireframe.

Confirm the brief before creating the editable HTML wireframe. A confirmed style brief authorizes schematic scientific placeholders inside a production-binding layout-and-style wireframe; it does not authorize formal poster production.
